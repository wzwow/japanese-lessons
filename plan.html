Learning plan: beginner to advanced

A 5-stage route from first greetings to running client meetings in Japanese, written for a designer who works with Japanese clients and collaborators. It runs on 15–20 minutes a day, 7 days a week.

How the plan works

Rhythm: one lesson a day. Days 1–6 of each week bring new material (4–5 phrases, 5–6 extra vocab, one grammar point). Day 7 is a weekly review lesson, so lessons 7, 14, 21 and so on are always reviews. Week n covers lessons 7n−6 to 7n.

Blend: each week mixes everyday Japanese (greetings, food, travel, small talk) with design-work Japanese (reviews, revisions, files, deadlines, presenting), and every lesson ends with a short designer–client dialogue.

A realistic note on time. 15–20 minutes a day comes to about 110 hours a year. That's enough for Stages 1–2 on schedule. JLPT's own estimates for N3 and N2 assume several hundred hours, though, so keeping to the Stage 3–5 timeline means adding about 15–20 minutes of Anki, listening and textbook work from Stage 3 onward (about 35 minutes a day in total). If you stay at 15–20 minutes, plan on Stages 3–5 taking roughly 1.5–2× as long. Reading Chinese characters makes kanji go much faster.

JLPT dates. The JLPT runs twice a year, on the first Sunday of July and of December, and it is also held at many overseas test sites. Registration closes months ahead, so check jlpt.jp for local dates. A suggested path: skip N5, sit N4 in July 2027 (after Stage 3), N3 in December 2027 (end of Stage 4), and N2 in 2028.

1FoundationsWeeks 1–6 · Lessons 1–42 · JLPT N5 · Genki I Ch. 1–5

Goals

Read all hiragana and katakana, get comfortable with polite です/ます Japanese, and handle a first meeting, a self-introduction and simple design comments.

Can-do statements

  • I can greet a client, introduce myself and my job, and exchange business cards.
  • I can read hiragana and katakana, including design loanwords (レイアウト, フォント).
  • I can ask what something is, and say what I like or don't like about a design in simple words.
  • I can understand and say times, dates and deadlines.
  • I can say what I did yesterday and what I'll do next week.

2Working beginnerWeeks 7–18 (months 2–4) · Lessons 43–126 · N5 → N4 · Genki I Ch. 4–12 → Genki II Ch. 13

Goals

Master the て-form and plain forms, make polite requests and give simple opinions, and take part in a slow, simple design review, mixing English where needed.

Can-do statements

  • I can ask a client to repeat, slow down, send files or wait.
  • I can describe work in progress and what's still not done.
  • I can give an opinion (〜と思います) and compare two design options.
  • I can explain the reason for a design choice in one sentence.
  • I can propose changes (大きくする, 白にする) and offer to do more (もう一案作りましょうか).
  • I can follow a slow, clear 5-minute conversation on familiar topics.

Lesson page design

This is the improved daily lesson layout. It keeps everything that works now (▶ play, 🐢 slow, 🎤 say-it-back, tap-a-word breakdowns, vocab sidebar) and adds the blocks below in this order.

① Progress bar (top): Stage 1 · Week 1 · Lesson 4 of 42 with a bar filled to the lesson, plus five stage pips. On review days it reads Week 1 review.
② Warm-up review (2 min): 3–4 phrases pulled from earlier lessons on a spaced schedule (see below). English shown first; tap to reveal the Japanese and audio, then self-rate 😊 / 😐 / 😕.
③ New phrases (4–5 cards): as now, Japanese, romaji, English, tappable word parts, and a usage note on when and to whom you'd say it (client vs colleague, formal vs casual).
④ Grammar point: one pattern per lesson. A pattern box (e.g. Noun を Verb-てください), a 2–3 sentence mini explanation, 2 extra examples, and a "common mistake" line.
⑤ Extra vocab sidebar (5–6 items): unchanged position (fixed right on wide screens, below on mobile), with audio.
⑥ Mini dialogue: 4–6 lines between designer (you) and client using today's phrases. Play-all button, plus a "you speak" mode where your lines are hidden and you say them with 🎤.
⑦ Quick quiz: 4–5 items mixing listen-and-choose meaning, English → choose Japanese, fill the particle, and order the words. Instant feedback, with the score saved.
⑧ Speak-back practice: 3 prompts in English ("Ask the client to check the draft"). Answer by voice and get a match score, with the target answer revealed after.
⑨ Finish: "Mark lesson complete" saves to localStorage, updates the progress bar, and links to tomorrow's lesson.

  • No new phrases. All 20–30 of the week's phrases appear in a shuffled listen → say → check loop.
  • One longer dialogue (8–10 lines) that combines the week's grammar in a single client scenario.
  • A 10-question quiz covering the week, plus 3 items from older weeks.
  • A "say it without looking" round: English prompt → 🎤 answer.

Review & spaced repetition

1. Built into the lessons

The warm-up block automatically pulls phrases from lessons 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days back (expanding intervals). Anything you rate 😕 comes back the next day, and 😊 pushes it to the next interval. Day-7 reviews consolidate the week, and the end-of-unit and end-of-stage checkpoints consolidate longer stretches.

2. Anki (5–10 min/day, from week 2)

  • One note per phrase and per vocab word, with the site's mp3 attached. Two cards each: Japanese → meaning (recognition) and English → say it aloud (production).
  • Settings: 10 new cards/day in Stages 1–2, rising to 15–20 in Stages 3–4. Never skip reviews; cut new cards instead when busy.
  • Kanji: from Stage 2, add kanji through words you already know (e.g. learn 確 via 確認), not as isolated characters.
  • Leeches (cards failed 8+ times): rewrite them with a design-work example sentence, or delete them.

3. Recycling in context

Each new lesson's dialogue deliberately reuses 2–3 phrases from earlier lessons (e.g. ご確認をお願いします returns in emails in Stage 4 as ご確認いただけますと幸いです), so old phrases grow into more polite versions instead of being dropped.

4. Monthly self-audit

On the last day of each month: re-record your self-introduction, re-take one old checkpoint quiz, and note three phrases you actually used at work. If accuracy on the old quiz falls below 80%, take a "catch-up week" (review lessons only) before going on.
